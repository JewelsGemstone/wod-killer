# 🍑 Glute Activation & Strength Blueprint for Better Back Squats

**Goal:** Improve glute activation, build posterior strength, and make back squats feel smoother and more powerful.  
**Athlete:** Julie St. John (5’4”, 48 yrs, 137 lb)  
**Current Squat:** 120 lb × 1 (0.88× BW)

---

## 🧠 Why Glutes Matter in the Squat

Your **gluteus maximus** is the prime mover for hip extension — driving you up out of the bottom of the squat.  
If your glutes aren’t firing properly, your quads and lower back take over, leading to:
- Sticking points halfway up  
- Forward lean or knee cave (valgus collapse)  
- Poor depth or loss of tension at the bottom  

**Goal:** Learn to “feel” the glutes first, then progressively overload them through smart accessory work.

---

## 🔥 1. Activate Glutes Before Squatting (Warm-Up)

Spend 5–7 minutes before every lower-body session on these:

| Exercise | Reps | Tips |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Glute Bridge (Bodyweight)** | 15 × 2 | Squeeze hard at top; feel your butt, not hamstrings. |
| **Clamshells (Mini Band)** | 15/side × 2 | Keep feet together; open knees slowly. |
| **Banded Lateral Walks (Mini Band at Ankles)** | 20 steps each way | Stay low; keep tension constant. |
| **Quadruped Kickbacks** | 12/side × 2 | Drive heel up and back, not just up. |

> 💡 *Cue to remember:* “Knees out, hips through, glutes on.”

---

## 🏋️‍♀️ 2. Technique Cues for Glute-Driven Back Squats

| Cue | Description |
|-----|--------------|
| **Foot placement:** | Slightly wider than shoulder-width, toes 15–30° out. Find stance where hips feel open. |
| **Tripod foot:** | Keep pressure evenly on heel, big toe, little toe — *no collapsing arches.* |
| **Brace first:** | Deep breath into belly → lock core before descent. |
| **Knees out:** | Push knees slightly out on the way down *and* up — this activates glutes and prevents valgus. |
| **Hip drive:** | Out of the bottom, think “push the floor apart” and “hips under the bar.” |
| **Finish tall:** | Squeeze glutes at the top; don’t over-arch your back. |

🎯 *Mental cue:*  
> “Sit between your heels, then drive the floor away with your glutes.”

---

## 🧱 3. Accessory Lifts to Build Glute Strength

Incorporate these 1–2× per week (after main lifts or on separate glute/conditioning day):

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Focus / Tip |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| **Hip Thrust (Barbell)** | 4×10–12 | King of glute isolation. Full hip lockout + pause. |
| **Bulgarian Split Squat** | 3×8/side | Slight forward lean; load glutes by going deep. |
| **Romanian Deadlift (RDL)** | 4×8 | Feel stretch in hamstrings + glutes; hinge, don’t squat. |
| **Step-Ups (Weighted)** | 3×10/side | Step to 90° knee angle; drive through heel. |
| **Cable Kickbacks** | 3×12/side | Isolate glute max; light weight, controlled. |
| **Walking Lunges** | 3×12/side | Long stride to bias glutes. |

Optional: **Glute Ham Raise** or **Reverse Hyperextension** if you have access to equipment.

## 🧩 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Letting knees cave in** → lose glute tension  
- **Rising hips first** → back takes over instead of glutes  
- **Not reaching depth** → glutes never fully activate  
- **Rushing reps** → glutes need control + tempo  
- **Ignoring recovery** → sore glutes can underperform

## 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Glutes lead, quads follow.** Train hip extension and external rotation deliberately.  
- **Feel before load.** If you don’t feel the glutes working, reduce weight and perfect tension.  
- **Warm up intentionally.** 5 minutes of activation improves every lift.  
- **Progress slowly.** Add load only when you maintain glute tension through the full range.
